Read left-to-right AND right-to-left

One to One (1:1)
A store has one manager A manager works at one store

One to Many (1:N)
A customer can have multiple orders An order belongs to one customer

Many to Many (M:N)
A student can enroll in many courses A course can have lots of students

Note! M:N cardinality requires a composite table (next slide)

The inner ones are minimum (0 or 1)
and the outer ones are maximum (1 or many)

Table ATable B
1 B or many B1 A or 1 A
0 B or many B1 A or many A
1 B or 1 B0 A or 1 A
0 B or 1 B0 A or many A

ta_name = “James”→ Exact match

ta_name != “James”→ Not exact match
ta_name <> “James”(these are equivalent)

ta_name IS NULL→ NULL means blank data (can also do IS NOT NULL)

ta_name LIKE ‘%ame%’→ The text (“ame”) is contained within the attribute
(% = wildcard)

ta_name IN (“James”, “Robert”, “Frankie”)
→ Exact match for any of these
